RESEARCH & ENGINEERING AUDIT

About Mess Queue Manager

Computational Thinking as an Engineering Mindset · Course SE-302A · Faculty: Dr. Sujithra · Jain (Deemed-to-be University).

01 · Problem Statement

Long lines. Empty counters.

During peak lunch rush (12:15 PM – 1:15 PM), the Jain University mess counters suffered from heavy queuing bottlenecks. National food counters had queues stretching 15–20 students deep, while adjacent international counters sat largely underutilized.

THE CROWD IMBALANCE

National counters (Counters 1–3) were consistently above 90% capacity, while International counters (Counters 4–5) stayed below 20% capacity.

LACK OF ADVANCE WARNING

Canteen kitchen staff were caught off guard when lines suddenly spiked, delaying the opening of backup standby counters by 6–10 minutes.

STUDENT VISIBILITY DEFICIT

Students walking in had no way of knowing which counter was fastest before committing to a 10+ minute queue.

02 · Engineering Mindset

The Four Pillars

How we applied core computational thinking principles to engineer an automated, robust queue balancing system.

D

DECOMPOSITION

Deconstructed canteen operations into discrete modules: queue sensing, zone average calculation, threshold triggers, cross-counter load balancing, and dispatch notification.

P

PATTERN RECOGNITION

Identified daily lunch rush surges peaking at 12:30 PM, the disparity between National and International throughput, and 30-second average serving time.

A

ABSTRACTION

Filtered out non-critical details (individual food preference, weather) and focused exclusively on queue count, serving time, capacity, and sold-out flags.

∞

ALGORITHMIC THINKING

Engineered precise deterministic threshold rules (45/50/80/40) with mathematical hysteresis to prevent rapid flapping between states.

03 · Threshold Logic

Rules with Hysteresis

Rules execute on the average crowd of open, available National counters to decide automated actions:

≥45%

EARLY ALERT

Triggers audio beep and vibration alert to staff. Kitchen prepares the standby counter with a 2-minute head start.

STAFF ALERT
≥50%

STANDBY OPENS

Standby counter automatically opens. Student screen routes traffic to Standby 1 immediately.

CAPACITY EXPANSION
>80%

CROSS-ACCESS

National students gain access to International counters with a 75% overflow safety cap and 15-minute Digital Express Pass.

OVERFLOW ROUTING
<40%

HYSTERESIS CLOSE

Standby closes and cross-access resets ONLY when crowd drops below 40%. The 40–45% deadband prevents state flapping.

STABLE RESET

STEP-BY-STEP ALGORITHM

  1. Read queue counts from all active counters.
  2. Compute crowd %: queue / capacity × 100.
  3. Compute wait time: queue × 30s / 60.
  4. Compute average crowd across National counters.
  5. If zone > 80%: Turn ON Cross-Access (cap int counters at 75%).
  6. Else if zone ≥ 50%: Open Standby counter.
  7. Else if zone ≥ 45%: Trigger 2-minute staff alert.
  8. Else if zone < 40%: Close standby and reset cross-access.
  9. Pick best counter with lowest wait and update student views.
