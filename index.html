01 · Problem statement

Long lines. Empty counters.

At lunch, the mess food counters get very crowded. Some counters are full while others sit empty. We need a simple, smart rule system that checks queue size and moves students to less crowded counters, so food is served faster and nobody waits too long.

OBJECTIVES

  • Reduce total waiting time for students getting food.
  • Use step-by-step logic to manage line capacity.
  • Open extra counters automatically during rush hours.
  • Show a clear flowchart and pseudocode for the process.
  • Collect and apply feedback from students and mess staff.

WHO USES IT

  • Students: scan the QR code, see the best counter, crowd % and wait time.
  • Mess staff: update crowd counts, receive sound/vibration alerts, open or close counters, mark sold-out items.
  • Admin (optional): change thresholds, capacity and serving time.

Runs as a web app on any phone or laptop browser, so there's nothing to install.

03 · Core logic

The rules

CROWD PERCENTAGE

crowd_% = (people_in_queue / capacity) × 100
// capacity 20, people 16 → 80%

ESTIMATED WAIT TIME

wait_min = (people × avg_serve_sec) / 60
// 16 × 30 s = 480 s → 8 minutes
≥45%

EARLY ALERT

Staff get a 2-minute sound & screen notice to prepare the standby counter.

STAFF
≥50%

OPEN STANDBY

The extra standby counter opens.

STAFF + STUDENTS
>80%

CROSS ACCESS

National students may use the international counters.

STAFF + STUDENTS
<40%

CLOSE / NORMAL

The standby counter closes and normal routing resumes.

STAFF
WHY HYSTERESIS? If a counter opened at 50% and closed at 49%, it would flip every minute while the crowd hovers around 50%. Closing only below 40% keeps things stable.
WHICH CROWD %? The rules use the average crowd of the national counters, which fill up first. Students still see each counter's own %.

05 · Algorithm

Step by step

STEPS

  1. Read the queue count of every counter.
  2. Calculate crowd % and wait time for each counter.
  3. Calculate the national zone average.
  4. Above 80%: cross-counter access ON and standby open.
  5. Else 50% or more: open the standby counter.
  6. Else 45% or more: send an early alert to staff.
  7. Below 40%: close standby and cross access.
  8. Choose the best open, non-sold-out counter and update the student screen.
  9. Wait 1–2 minutes and repeat.
START
LOOP every 1-2 minutes:
  FOR each counter:
    crowd_pct = queue / capacity * 100
    wait_min  = queue * avg_serve_sec / 60
  ENDFOR
  zone_avg = average crowd_pct of national counters

  IF zone_avg > 80 THEN
    cross_access = ON ; standby_open = ON
  ELSE IF zone_avg >= 50 THEN
    standby_open = ON
  ELSE IF zone_avg >= 45 THEN
    SEND alert "Prepare standby in 2 min"
  ELSE IF zone_avg < 40 THEN
    cross_access = OFF ; standby_open = OFF
  ENDIF

  best = open + allowed counter with lowest wait
  SHOW "Go to " + best.name
ENDLOOP
END
flowchart TD
    A[Start: read queue count of each counter] --> B[Calculate crowd % and wait time per counter]
    B --> C[Calculate national zone average]
    C --> D{Zone crowd > 80%?}
    D -- Yes --> E[Turn ON cross-counter access and open standby]
    D -- No --> F{Zone crowd >= 50%?}
    F -- Yes --> G[Open standby counter]
    F -- No --> H{Zone crowd >= 45%?}
    H -- Yes --> I[Alert staff: 2-minute notice]
    H -- No --> J{Zone crowd < 40%?}
    J -- Yes --> K[Close standby and cross access]
    J -- No --> L[Keep current state]
    E --> M[Pick best counter by lowest wait]
    G --> M
    I --> M
    K --> M
    L --> M
    M --> N[Update student screens]
    N --> O[Wait 1-2 minutes]
    O --> A

06 · Computational thinking

The four pillars

D

DECOMPOSITION

Split the problem into measuring queues, checking thresholds, opening counters, directing students, and notifying staff.

P

PATTERN RECOGNITION

National counters fill up at lunch while international ones stay free. The rush peaks at 12:30 PM.

A

ABSTRACTION

Track only queue count, capacity, sold-out state, and serving time. Ignore individual meal details.

∞

ALGORITHMIC THINKING

Fixed IF/ELSE rules (45/50/80/40), repeated on a timer.
